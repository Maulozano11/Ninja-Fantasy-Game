# Earth set v3 (`vfx_earth_v3`): copied from the owner's earth sheet

The owner repainted our earth kit sheet ("new earth set") and asked for it to be copied as exactly as possible. Every sprite's key frame is his picture, cut from his sheet at game scale. The other frames are derived from that key frame. The stone beast is out of scope: `beast.png` and its manifest entry are copied through from `vfx_earth` unchanged (byte-identical).

This is the seventh fix pass. It answers the review of round 5, third pass (`review-earth-set-r5-3/`). Frame counts, fps and anchor meanings are unchanged from the first fix pass (the owner's "too fast, like a flashing star" pass, `frames-brief.md`). My working files for this pass are in `earth-r8/`: measuring scripts in `earth-r8/py/`, the group before this pass in `earth-r8/before/`, and the generator before it in `earth-r8/vfx-earth-v3.ts.before`.

## Renderer notes

- **Place everything by the manifest anchor.** bolt, stream, stream_head, breath and slash changed frame size in this pass, and stream, stream_head and breath their anchor (same meaning).
- **Stream:** tiles every 64 px, anchor (0, 61). The head's anchor (32, 61) goes at the end of the line of tiles. Its first 32 columns repeat the last tile's right half, so the head must play on the last tile's frame. The renderer change for this is being made in `src` by the orchestrator: tiles step back from the head a whole tile at a time, all on the head's frame, the first tile clipped at the caster.
- **`multi_hit`:** plays at the bullet's impact point (the `burst` case) and is mirrored for bullets flying left. In his picture the rays meet on the right of the fan: the dust kicks back toward the shooter and the broken chunks fly on.
- **Aura:** `aura_back` goes behind the caster and `aura` over them. They share one frame size and anchor, and their 12 frames are in step.
- **Walls:** the anchor is the footprint's centre on the ground (`drawWallSprite` places a wall at the midpoint of its two ends). `rise` plays once (12 frames), then `idle` loops.

## Known differences from his picture

1. **Size.** His projectile panels are about 1.6x their labelled 64x64 at character scale. I followed his art, so the frames grew.
2. **Resolution.** At 1/2.35 his one-pixel cracks and leaf speckles merge. His moss strip on the sphere is a little thinner than his.
3. **Dust.**
   - Dust is semi-transparent. On his dark fill it looks like his.
   - On a bright map his darker dust folds read lighter.
   - The blast's ground shadow is a semi-transparent stain.
4. **Unseen dust.** The dust behind his crystals, boulder and spikes is never shown in his picture. It is filled from his dust round them, so it is smoother than his. Behind his outer blast crystals it thins out over a few px.
5. **Cut edges.** His panel cut the bolt's trail (now continued past the cut and faded there), the blast's cloud and the end-on wall's side, and his figure cut the blast's shadow and two dust streaks. They end in jittered wisps, or (the wall) in an outlined edge broken back course by course.
6. **Motion is mine.** His pictures are stills.
   - The boulder rocks ±6° (keeping his moss on top).
   - The crystals rise out of and sink into the cloud: in the dust their feet end raggedly in it, outside it on a soft line. Their last tips pale out.
   - The walls push up out of the ground from below it (the end-on pillar and its heap as one block); the loose pebbles land at the end.
   - The stream's spikes squash toward their feet and stretch back; its flying chunks drop and rise whole.
   - The rocks of the one-shots wear away as compact stones and their last remnants pale out; his marks on the dust pale into it.

8. **Aura.** His figure is removed from his panel, and the band across his waist is rebuilt along his line. The tan dust puff beside his right leg, inside the right column, is copied as solid paint, as he drew it.
