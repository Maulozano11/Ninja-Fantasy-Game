# Lightning jutsu VFX, v3: built from the owner's paint-over

The owner asked for his examples to be copied as exactly as possible. So every sprite's key frame here is his own picture (`REF-lightning-set-owner.jpg`), cut out at game scale and cleaned up into pixel art. The other animation frames are derived from that key frame.

- **Group:** `vfx_lightning_v3` in `staging/vfx_lightning_v3/`: one PNG per sprite plus `manifest.json`, tag `vfx`.
  - Sprite names, anim names, frame counts, fps and loop flags are the same as in `vfx_lightning`.
  - Anchors mean the same things as before.
- **`beast` is not in this group.** The raijū belongs to the beasts artist. At integration, keep whatever `beast` is already there.
- **Merging:** copy the 13 PNGs and the manifest entries into `vfx_lightning`. Frame sizes and anchor numbers changed (see the table below), so the manifest entries must come along with the PNGs.

Build:

```bash
# only when changing the cut:
python3 -I <art2>/vfx-lightning-v3-extract.py <art2> tools/world-sprites/vfx-lightning-v3-refdata.ts [previewDir]
WORLD_OUT=<art2>/staging npx tsx tools/world-sprites/vfx-lightning-v3.ts
python3 -I <art2>/vfx-lightning-v3-review.py <art2>
```

The generator has these safeguards:
- It refuses to run without `WORLD_OUT`.
- It writes the group into a temp folder and renames the files in, PNGs first and the manifest last.
- It is deterministic: two builds are byte-identical (checked).
- It throws if any frame has art on its border.

It takes about 3 s. `kit.ts` and every `vfx-a*` / `vfx-b*` / `vfx-sig-*` / `vfx-beasts` file are untouched.

## How the key frames were made

1. **Scale 1:1.** His character is 96 px tall (y 319–414 beside the blast), the same as ours at 1x. His panels also have our frame sizes (bolt 74x64, slash 64x72, multi 72x64, breath 106x113). So one of his pixels is one game pixel.
   - Each panel's inner top-left maps to our old frame's (0, 0).
   - The anchors were checked against his features: the bolt's core, the sphere's centre, the blast's impact, the walls' post feet, the aura's feet.
2. **Cleanup.**
   - Removed: his panel background (#20202a), his characters, the labels and the panel borders.
   - The JPEG is lightly smoothed before every decision.
   - Where his character hid part of a ground strip, the gap is filled by diffusion from the pixels around it. This covers the blast stain under his feet, the 3/4 wall's strip behind him, and the column glow behind his cape on the end-on wall.
3. **Classes:**
   - **Stroke:** opaque white, cyan and light blue arcs.
   - **Outline:** a 1 px deep-blue ring (#1c3aa8) round every stroke. Indigo (#262268) is used round the sphere's rings. No black anywhere.
   - **Body:** the sphere's opaque orb.
   - **Cream:** the sphere's rings.
   - **Spark:** his little '+' sparks, redrawn crisp with no outline.
   - **Glow:** his soft glow, kept semi-transparent. It is colour-to-alpha'd off his background, so over a dark ground it composites back to his exact look.
   - **Ground:** the blast's stain and the 3/4 wall's strip. Also un-blended, but with an alpha floor of 0.55, so it reads as a stain on any map, not only as light.
4. **Palette.** One palette for the set, median-cut from his pixels of each class (40 entries):
   - outline `#1c3aa8` `#262268`
   - stroke `#3a85c4` `#648ebb` `#62b2de` `#92bde0` `#90dff5` `#b0e7f8` `#c5effa` `#c9fbfd` `#defcfd` `#eaffff`
   - cream `#645c53` `#8c8680` `#b4ada4` `#d8d5cd`
   - spark `#7b6d57` `#a79b7d` `#d2d2af`
   - body `#15224d` `#1a3672` `#2659ae` `#2269d3` `#376bb8`
   - glow: 9 blues, alpha 26–154
   - ground: 7 blues, alpha 140–196

## How the other frames are derived

The owner then asked for more, slower frames: "too fast... it looks like a flashing star". So every in-between now moves a little and continuously, and nothing flashes. Every effect is exactly nothing at the key frame, so the key frames are still his picture, pixel-identical to the approved build (checked).

The generator works on the indexed key frames, so every frame stays in the palette. It uses these operations:
- **churn:** the arcs drift along a smooth random field that circles once per loop (about a pixel per frame), so they re-route without jumping.
- **shimmer:** soft waves of ±1 brightness step drift across the art, each region at its own time, so the whole sprite never brightens at once.
- **twinkle:** each whole spark goes out for part of the cycle, at its own time (never on frame 0).
- **dim:** the "ladder": white, blue, outline blue, glow, gone. Light dies this way instead of greying out.
- **scale-about / rotate-about:** grows an effect from its centre, or sways the sphere's rings.

| anim | frames | fps | timeline |
|---|---|---|---|
| `fly` (bolt, stream, stream_head, breath, sphere, slash, multi) | 8 | 12 | Frame 0 his picture, then churn, shimmer and twinkle (one cycle per loop). The core never moves. The sphere's rings sway ±3.4°. The multi's needle heads hold. The stream flows 8 px a frame and still tiles; the head's first 26 columns are the same frame of the tile. |
| `hit` | 12 | 12 | 0–2: builds up (his burst at 50%, 71%, 87%). 3: his picture. 4–5: held, churning. 6–11: draws in and cools while the spikes fly out and the sparks drift off. |
| `blast` | 16 | 12 | 0–3: anticipation (the charge high up; the leader at 20%, 50%, 80% with a hot tip while the ground starts to glow; touchdown). 4: his picture. 5–6: held, churning. 7–15: a long settle (the bolt dies from the top down, the ground crackle cools, sparks fall, the scorch stays). |
| `rise` (wall, wall_diag, wall_depth) | 12 | 12 | The posts (or end-on columns) grow out of the ground with hot tips, then the arcs crawl out from the posts, then the strip glows up. Frame 11 is his picture, the same as idle frame 0. |
| `idle` (wall, wall_diag, wall_depth) | 8 | 10 | His picture, then churn (pinned at the posts), shimmer and twinkle. |
| aura `idle` | 12 | 10 | His picture, then churn, shimmer and twinkle over the coordinator's breathing veil. |

**Flash check** (`vfx-lightning-v3-energy.txt`): the light per frame changes smoothly everywhere.
- Every loop's biggest step between neighbouring frames, including the wrap, is at most 3.6% (it was 5–20% with the 4-frame loops).
- The one-shots ramp evenly:
  - hit: 26, 52, 73, 97, 97, 100, 96, 84, 63, 42, 21, 6;
  - blast: 6, 20, 45, 75, 100, 100, 100, 86 … 15;
  - rises: steps of at most 17%.

**Kept from the coordinator:** the aura's `veil()` (the blue veil over the body; it breathes about twice a loop and loops seamlessly at 12 frames) and the 3/4 wall's `softGround()` (the softened ground strip) are unchanged.

## For the renderer

- **Place everything by the manifest anchor.** Every frame size and anchor number changed, but not what the anchors mean.
- **Blending:**
  - Draw with normal alpha blending (source-over), nearest-neighbour, at integer scale.
  - The glow and the ground strips are semi-transparent. Their colours were un-blended from a dark background, so over dark ground they look exactly like his sheet, and over bright ground they read as blue light.
  - Don't use additive blending on these sheets: it would double the glow.
- **Blast:**
  - The bolt comes down from about 3.7 m (213 px) above the anchor. Its top fades into its own glow over about 21 px, because his bolt ran off the top of his panel.
  - His strike is wider than the old 3 m ellipse. The stain and crackle span about -136 ... +165 px across the anchor (about 5.3 m), and lie mostly in front of the impact (down to +68 px).
- **3/4 wall (`wall_diag`):**
  - His fence carries a dense mass of arcs about 95 px beyond its far post, and a broad blue ground strip that runs under and past the character. Both are kept.
  - So the art is off-centre toward the far end: about -115 ... +176 px across the anchor, and -183 ... +53 px down.
  - For "\" mirror it horizontally.
- **Aura:**
  - Draw it over the character with its anchor on the feet. The middle (our character's silhouette, plus 2 px) is clear, apart from a few bright arcs crossing the legs.
- **Stream:**
  - Repeat the 64x64 tile along the line with the centre line on y = 32.
  - Put `stream_head` at the far end, its left edge touching the last tile, and play every piece on the same frame index.
  - Rotate the tiles and the head together.

## Checks

- Typecheck: `npx tsc --noEmit -p . 2>&1 | grep vfx-lightning-v3` prints nothing. Both v3 files are in the project.
- Border check, every frame of every sprite (`vfx-lightning-v3-border.txt`): 0 frames with art on the border. The exceptions are the stream tile's left and right edges, by design, and the head's joint edge.
- Determinism: two builds into different folders are byte-identical (`diff -r`).

## Where it still differs from his picture

1. **Outlines.**
   - The rule is a 1 px deep-blue ring round every stroke. His painting has softer transitions, and in places near-black JPEG ringing, which was not copied.
   - So his thin white-grey spikes (`hit`, the breath's spray, the blast's dashes) read bluer in ours.
2. **Sparks.** His little '+' sparks were blurred into blobs by the JPEG, so they are redrawn as crisp '+' shapes at his positions. The JPEG's 4:2:0 chroma subsampling had greyed them out, so their chroma was doubled. They are still a pale beige-gold like his, not the saturated yellow of v2.
3. **What his characters hid** (the blast stain under his feet, the 3/4 strip behind him, the end-on columns' glow behind his cape) is filled smoothly from around it. It has no pixel detail of his there.
4. **Aura.** His figure was tinted blue under the glow. Ours keeps the middle clear for readability: only his arcs around and across the body remain, not the glow over it.
5. **Edges where his art ran off his panel** fade out:
   - the blast bolt's top;
   - the 3/4 wall's top and right arcs;
   - the 3/4 wall's ground strip, over 56 px.
6. **Glow.** His soft gradients are quantized into 9 glow steps. A few desaturated light blues (`#648ebb`, `#92bde0`), sampled from his aura and end-on wall, flicker slightly grey when the strokes brighten.
7. **The in-between frames are derived, not drawn.** The hit's first three frames are his burst scaled down (nearest-neighbour), so the smallest is blockier than a hand-drawn small burst. The blast leader's front is a ragged cut of his bolt with a hot tip, not a drawn leader.
8. **The scale is 1:1 with his JPEG,** which is itself 1x. There was no finer detail to recover.
