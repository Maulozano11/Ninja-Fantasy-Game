# Poison set v3: copied from the owner's paint-over

The owner repainted our poison sheet (`REF-poison-set-owner.jpg`, also `docs/reference/poison-set-owner-reference.jpg`) and asked for it to be copied as exactly as possible. Every sprite's key frame in `vfx_poison_v3` is his picture (except the blast's light: see **Blast light**). The other frames are derived from it, with the new, slower timing.

Two exceptions:
- **wall_depth is ours.** He said his end-on wall is not what he wants, so our mist curtain is kept, played with the new frame counts.
- **beast is out of scope.** Another artist does the cobra. `beast.png` and its manifest entry are copied through from `vfx_poison` unchanged (136x96, `fly` 6 frames at 12 fps, byte-identical).

- **Group:** `vfx_poison_v3`, written to `staging/vfx_poison_v3/` (one PNG per sprite plus `manifest.json`). Every sprite is tagged `vfx`.
- **Sprite and anim names:** the same as `vfx_poison`, plus `aura_back` (see the aura below).
- **Staging and source are in sync:** the staged files are exactly what the current repo source builds (two builds byte-identical with the staged ones; the extractor rebuilds the committed refdata byte for byte).

## How it is made

1. **Scale.** His sheet is his paint-over of our 2x sheet, brought back to about 1x. Our character (kit `characterRef()`, 96 px tall) registered on his four characters (beside the blast, the wall, the 3/4 wall and in the aura) fits at 1.00 of his px across and 1.02 down, so one of his pixels is 1.0 x 0.98 game pixels. His characters measure 95 px tall by their bounding boxes. The stream is the exception: his beam repeats every 65.9 of his px (it was our 64 px tile), so the stream and its head are read at 64/65.9 across and one period is exactly one tile.
2. **Extractor: `tools/refs/poison-from-ref.py`.** For each of his panels it:
   - reads only the inside of his panel borders, which removes his labels and borders;
   - removes his characters (our character registered on his, grown by 3 px) and their cast shadows;
   - smooths the JPEG noise before every decision (colours from a lightly sharpened copy, dark rims from the unsmoothed one, chroma smoothed within each colour family, single-pixel specks cleaned);
   - classifies each pixel at his resolution, then box-filters to game scale:
     - opaque body, quantized to one palette for the set from his own pixels (each sprite gets a fair share): 52 greens (slime), 14 violets (venom), 24 neutrals (stone, bone, pale motes);
     - a clean 1 px tinted outline wherever his art has a dark rim: deep green `#10280d` round the slime, dark violet `#25182d` round the venom, dark stone `#191f1b` round the frames, never black;
     - his soft light and the soft edges of his mist, kept semi-transparent (colour-to-alpha against his panel colour): 10 hues from his pixels, 14 alpha steps each;
   - **the blast's light is read straight from his pixels**: his green excess over his panel colour (his light lies on one line from his panel colour, so it is one colour, `#6ce957`) with full 8-bit alpha, stored apart (zlib). Its zone is everything of his light the open light reaches without crossing his slime or its rim, so it runs right up to the pool's rim (no opaque body outside the rim). Only his character and its cast shadow are filled, together with the gaps his limbs close round (between the legs, between an arm and the body: his light seen through them is brighter than the light round the figure and made a bright spot with ridges rising from it). The fill is the solution of Laplace's equation meeting his light round it, solved to convergence (conjugate gradients), so it has no bright spot, no ridge and no point brighter than his light round it. At his panel's bottom his light fades first (each column over 8-24 px by its own brightness), and where his shadow and the dark rock his character stands on run out to his panel's edge and bottom (his picture is dark there) the fill meets his dark (0), so it never forms a bright core where his picture is dark (on the rows where his light runs bright right up to the edge, beside his character, it meets his light instead, so no dark notch is cut into the edge). Under his character's feet the light rounds off in one elliptical cap, from the right end of his light (the last row it reaches his panel's edge on) down to the panel's bottom at the shadow's left side, so the light's lower-right corner is round, not a square box (only the fill and the run-on past his edge are touched; it eases in over 6 rows). **His pixels keep their full brightness right up to his panel's right edge**; only past it does the light fade out: each row runs on from his edge column, blending over 6 px into his edge light smoothed over rows (sigma 10 px), and falls (smoothstep) to nothing at its reach, which is one elliptical cap over all the rows his light reaches the edge on (16 px at its middle, less above and below), so the light's right end is one convex curve, rounded top and bottom, with no lumps. (The generator now uses this light only for its level at the pool's right edge and to sort his glows; the light it draws is the pool's ground glow, see **Blast light**.);
   - **the stone frames are solid**: enclosed see-through pockets in the frame pieces (his dark moulding keyed out with his panel colour) are filled with his stone (400 px in the wall, 706 in the 3/4 wall);
   - behind the aura's figure, his flames are filled in along the flames (each column takes his flame above the hole, folded; the sides run on mirrored), never with a flat colour; his thin flame strands carry no dark rim (no dark specks on grass);
   - cuts each key frame into pieces the generator animates (main, drops, glow, and per sprite: the bolt's and breath's mist, the sphere's ring, the slash's dashes, the hit's streaks, the blast's pool and mist, the walls' stone frame in three blocks: each post and the base beam), and writes `tools/world-sprites/vfx-poison-v3-refdata.ts`.
3. **Generator: `tools/world-sprites/vfx-poison-v3.ts`.** It decodes the pieces and only derives frames from them: turning (the bolt's coil, the sphere's ring), churning his slime inside its own silhouette, swaying, drifting drops, scrolling (the stream), pushing up and sinking, re-rimming, cooling down his own colour ramps in patches, dimming his light and fading. The peak frame is always his picture, pixel for pixel, except the blast's light, which is the ground glow (see **Blast light**). wall_depth is our mist-curtain code from `vfx-b-poison.ts`, copied into the generator; `vfx-b-poison.ts` was not edited. It refuses to write if any frame has art on its border, any see-through pixel is left in the stone, a post top changes shape during a rise, a blast frame has an enclosed see-through pixel (alpha under 128 that the outside cannot reach), a hole in its light (see **Blast light** below) or a straight violet top (more than 4 neighbouring columns sharing their topmost violet row), the blast's moving crown runs flat over more than 4 columns, or the aura's two layers together leave a see-through pixel in his pool or deep inside his flames.
4. **Review: `tools/refs/poison-v3-review.py`.** It makes the compare sheet, the animation strips, the GIFs and the border, see-through-stone, flash and light checks.

## Blast light

**The side glow is the pool's glow on the ground (owner's request).** His light, read from his pixels and continued behind his removed character, had become a block to the right of the pool: a straight top, a straight right edge, a flat wall of light above the ground (its right edge ran straight for 25-37 px). In his picture that light is the pool lighting the ground and his character's legs, so it is now one soft ellipse on the ground plane (`groundGlow` in the generator), in his light's colour `#6ce957`:

- **Centre and level.** Centred 28 px inside the pool's right end (its rightmost column, x 193, on the rows that reach it: centre 166,119), so it wraps that end of the pool and is brightest at the pool's edge, at his light's level there (his light over the 8 px just outside the pool's right end, 7 rows: alpha 149, drawn on his step 144).
- **Falloff.** By the ellipse's radius r past the pool's edge (d): exp(-S A (sqrt(1 + (d/A)^2) - 1)) with A 0.2, S 5.6: level at the pool's edge, then falling by a share of at most S per unit of r, so its faint outer bands are as wide as its bright ones. Radii: 79 px to the right (it ends at x 244, inside his panel's edge at 245, where his own light ended), 48 up the far ground, 43 down the near ground, 40 to the left (under the pool). Its faintest step (alpha 10) ends on the ellipse's rim. Under the pool it stays at the edge's level, so the ground the pool leaves as it breaks up is lit like the ground round it, with no bright core and no dark ghost.
- **Stepped.** Each frame puts it on his light's own alpha steps (10, 20, 32, 45, 59, 74, 90, 107, 125, 144), so it falls off in soft elliptical bands. Where his art and the 144 band close off a bit of the 125 band (the see-through line, alpha 128, falls between those two steps), that bit takes the light round it (1, 2 and 3 px on frames 4-6), so no dim slit is left enclosed.
- **Size against his.** Frame 4's glow covers x 135-244, y 71-161 (4838 px); his own light (alpha 5 or more, outside his removed character, inside his panel) covered x 133-245, y 25-162. Its outline has no straight edge: the longest straight run on its right side is 6-10 px (at the ellipse's rightmost point; the block's was 25-37), the longest flat run on its top 9-12 px (the faint apex of a 119 px wide ellipse). Its total light on frame 4 is about a third of the block's.
- **In time.** It grows with the pool (scaled with the pool about the anchor, dimmed with the light) and dims in the settle: its own light per frame, in % of its peak, 15 35 60 86 99 100 96 89 78 67 57 48 46 37 21 6 (the block's was 14 33 58 83 100 100 99 92 83 72 60 48 37 25 13 5); its brightest step per frame 59, 90, 125, 144 (frames 3-7), then 125, 104, 84, 64, 47, 31, 17, 4.
- **His glows.** His green glow inside his old light (round the pool's right end, the stalks and their drops: his light read as glow at his slime's edge) takes the ground glow's share of his light there, so it goes where the ground glow does not reach (no green halo round the stalks over unlit ground); it is drawn only where the frame shows his art unchanged and in place round it. His glow round the pool's rim outside his light lies over the ground glow on frame 4 and goes with the pool's slime after.
- **Frame 4** is his picture everywhere but the light: no pixel of his art at alpha 200 or more differs from last round's frame 4; only the light and the soft (semi-transparent) pixels lying over it changed.
- **Build check.** On every blast frame, a pixel fails when it is not slime (alpha under 200), the strongest pixel of the light's own colour within 3 px is alpha 24 or more and over twice its alpha, and along some direction (across, down or a diagonal) something over twice its alpha (light or slime) lies on both sides of it within 3 px: a hole or an enclosed pocket in the light. The build fails on any. Result: 0 on all 16 frames (the review tool counts it again: 0 on all 16). The falloff above was chosen for it: a gentler-shouldered ellipse (36 px up) left 1-19 px per frame where its faint bands pinched against the pool's back rim and the drips.

## Flash check

The light of a frame is its mean alpha × brightness (per mille). The last column is the largest change between neighbouring frames, in percent of the animation's peak; for loops it includes the step from the last frame back to the first. No frame is brighter than both neighbours by more than 10 % of the peak (no pop) anywhere.
